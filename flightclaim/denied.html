Before acting: This page concerns European Union air passenger rights, not every international flight. Check the flight's operating carrier, route, final destination, booking and reason for disruption. We are not an airline, regulator, claims agency or law firm.

1. Check which claim you made

Ticket refunds, statutory compensation for disruption, and reimbursement of reasonable care expenses are separate issues with separate conditions. Make sure the airline replied to the actual request you submitted.

EU official eligibility and complaint guide

2. Preserve the evidence

Keep the booking confirmation, relevant flight and final-arrival times, cancellation/change notice, original claim, airline refusal explanation, and any expenses with receipts. Do not post full bookings or identity documents publicly.

View free example evidence pack

3. Consider official escalation

The EU guide says to complain to the airline first. If the airline has not replied within 2 months or the response is unsatisfactory, a passenger can approach the relevant national enforcement authority. Different authorities have different roles and procedures.

European Commission: national enforcement authorities

Free self-check before considering any service

  • Which specific right did you request: airfare refund, fixed compensation, or expense reimbursement?
  • Was the disruption on a route covered by EU rules, and which airline actually operated the flight?
  • When did you reach your final destination (not simply take off or land at a connection)?
  • What reason did the airline give, and what evidence supports or contradicts the timeline?
  • Have you received an airline response, or has 2 months passed without one?

Answers can remain private. No guarantee of success follows from any particular answer; official bodies and airlines evaluate each case individually.
