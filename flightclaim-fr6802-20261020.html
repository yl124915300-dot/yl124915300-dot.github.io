If you held a booking on this flight

  1. Keep your evidence privately. Preserve the itinerary, cancellation message and its received time, airline correspondence, re-routing offers and choices, final arrival details if you travelled, and itemised reasonable expense receipts. The snapshot above is supporting information; it does not prove an individual's booking or notice time.
  2. Contact Ryanair directly, for free. Use My Bookings or the official cancellation guidance to discuss refund or re-routing and any necessary care. Ask the airline to arrange assistance before making alternative arrangements; retain its response.
  3. Assess compensation separately. This Spain-departing flight is within the EU261 geographic scope. A payment cannot be confirmed without notice timing, cancellation cause, re-routing details and the passenger's final journey outcome. Use the free FlightClaim checker with your own facts. Unknown facts must stay unknown. See EU passenger-rights guidance.
  4. Submit through the airline's own channels. Ryanair links to an EU261 compensation request and a separate expenses request. These may require your own airline login. Enter private booking information only with the airline; save the submission receipt.
  5. Escalate if necessary. For a journey starting in Spain, AESA's free ADR procedure for flights from 2 June 2023 onward is the relevant route. First complain to the airline. After an unsatisfactory reply, or one month without a reply, you may apply to AESA within one year of that first airline complaint. If your single-booking journey began elsewhere, check the competent authority first. Submit required documents directly to AESA.
